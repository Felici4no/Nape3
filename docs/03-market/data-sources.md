# Data sources: what exists, what is legitimate

Research date: 2026-10-08. Several platform sites were not reachable from the
research environment, so some facts come from search excerpts and press.
Anything marked **unverified** must be checked by hand before it is quoted
in the pitch.

## Conclusion

**No delivery platform offers an API that returns what a consumer sees at
checkout** (item price as shown in the app, delivery fee, service fee,
promotions) for stores other than your own. Every official API is
merchant-side: a restaurant authorizes a software provider, and the API moves
*that* restaurant's menu and orders between its POS and the marketplace.

So cart-level comparison stays an **observation** problem. The defensible
sources are:

1. what users see in their own sessions (the extension, observe-only);
2. ground truth shared by partner restaurants;
3. merchant APIs (Open Delivery, Keeta, iFood) as the *merchant-verified
   price* roadmap, not as competitor data.

See [ADR-007](../decisions/ADR-007-no-headless-scraping.md) for why a
dedicated account plus a headless scraper is out.

## Keeta (Meituan)

- Pilot in Santos / São Vicente from 30 Oct 2025; São Paulo and 8 metro
  cities from 1 Dec 2025. Stated goal: 100 cities by mid-2026. Actual 2026
  footprint **unverified**.
  ([Mercado & Consumo](https://mercadoeconsumo.com.br/09/10/2025/foodservice/keeta-estreia-no-brasil-com-operacao-piloto-no-litoral-paulista/),
  [Canaltech](https://canaltech.com.br/apps/keeta-comeca-a-operacao-em-sao-paulo-nesta-segunda-1o-com-ate-r-200-em-cupons/))
- **The Keeta API is a merchant / software-provider integration API**, not a
  price-reading API ([docs](https://api-docs.mykeeta.com)):
  - an Open Delivery variant ("based on Open Delivery v1.5.0"): menu push
    (`POST /merchantUpdate`), menu pull (`GET /merchant`), orders, events;
  - a Standard API with AppId, a secret "supplied by the Keeta team", OAuth 2.0;
  - credentials are per software provider, and each merchant must authorize
    it through a login portal;
  - access starts with an **NDA** with Keeta.
- Consumer ordering appears to be app-only (no web storefront confirmed).

## Open Delivery (Abrasel)

- Open, Apache-2.0 REST standard:
  [spec v1.7.1](https://github.com/Abrasel-Nacional/opendelivery); v2 in
  [design](https://github.com/Abrasel-Nacional/opendelivery-v2).
- Roles: Software Service (the restaurant's POS), Ordering Application (the
  marketplace), Logistic Service, hubs.
- The merchant's POS serves `GET /v1/merchant` (info, services, menus);
  credentials are "generated BY MERCHANT".
- Implemented by 99Food and Keeta
  ([Giro News](https://gironews.com/food-service/open-delivery-99food-e-keeta-se-integram-a-iniciativa-da-abrasel/)),
  Rappi (2022 announcement) and 100+ POS vendors. iFood: **unverified**.
- **What it gives an aggregator:** with each restaurant's opt-in, its base
  menu and prices. **What it does not give:** each marketplace's price, fees
  or promotions.

This fits UPAY3FOOD as a *merchant-verified* layer: a connector that reads
`GET /v1/merchant` from opted-in restaurants and compares their own price with
what users observe on each marketplace.

## Public pages and routing (founder check, 2026-10-10)

iFood shop pages and menus can be browsed **without logging in** once an
address area is set. This has two consequences:

- the extension's menu views work for anyone, logged in or not;
- a recommendation can send any user to a shop or item link, and on phones
  that link opens the iFood app.

The same routing will point to other platforms (Keeta, 99Food, Rappi) when
they are the cheapest. UPAY3FOOD still does not fetch those pages from a
server (ADR-007); it only links to them.

## Other legitimate sources

- **Partner restaurants:** a few açaí shops listed on 2–3 platforms share
  their per-platform prices and fees. Cheap, consented, ground truth.
- **Google Places API:** place attributes only, no item menus. Useful for
  matching the same store across platforms, not for prices.
- **Affiliate programs:** none found for iFood, Keeta or 99Food.

## Legal context (summary, not legal advice)

- Consumer ToS clauses were **not retrieved**. Read the current terms of each
  platform for "automatizado", "robô", "coleta", "engenharia reversa" and
  "uso pessoal" before the pitch.
- iFood's partner, courier and developer terms prohibit reverse engineering
  ([developer terms](https://developer.ifood.com.br/pt-BR/terms-of-use/developer/)).
- No Brazilian precedent specific to price scraping was found. Theories cited
  by commentators: unfair competition (LPI art. 195), copyright, and LGPD when
  personal data is involved
  ([Demarest](https://www.demarest.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Web-scraping-legal-ou-ilegal-1.pdf)).
- The sector is litigious:
  - iFood sued Meituan / Keeta alleging corporate espionage
    ([Startups](https://startups.com.br/negocios/food-delivery/ifood-acusa-meituan-de-esquema-de-espionagem-corporativa/));
  - in May 2026, 99Food was convicted for unlawful comparative advertising
    ("Taxômetro") ([Olhar Digital](https://olhardigital.com.br/2026/05/07/pro/justica-condena-99food-por-campanha-contra-ifood/)).
    **Any public comparison UPAY3FOOD shows must be dated, observed and
    accurate.**
- CADE's 2023 settlement limits iFood exclusivity
  ([Tecnoblog](https://tecnoblog.net/noticias/ifood-esta-proibido-de-fechar-exclusividade-com-grandes-redes-decide-cade/)).
  More restaurants on several apps (multi-homing) is the premise of the
  comparison. It is a market argument, not a data right.
